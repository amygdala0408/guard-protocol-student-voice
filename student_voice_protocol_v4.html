Pre-Interview Bias Mitigation

Non-negotiable. Complete before substantive interview. Purpose is not to change your belief — it is to notice what you already believe so you can recognize it during the interview. These answers remain confidential and are not shared with the team.

Cultural Validity — Interviewer Self-Preparation

Complete in writing before the interview. Keep confidential. Direct eye contact, silence, and verbal expressiveness all vary by cultural norm. "Cool pose," minimal affect, and over-compliance may be protective strategies, not dishonesty or lack of feeling.

Consent Protocol & Threat Assessment Routing

Read aloud, verbatim:

"I want to talk with you about what happened on [date of incident]. I'm here to listen to YOUR side of the story — what you were experiencing, what was happening around you, and what you think happened. This is NOT about getting you in trouble. It's about making sure the adults understand your perspective. Everything you tell me is going to be shared with the team that decides what happens next. You don't have to answer every question. If there's something you don't want to talk about, you can say so, and we'll move on. You can also ask me to explain a question if you don't understand it. Is it okay if we talk about this?"

If a threat assessment is also in progress, add verbatim:

"If you tell me something that makes me worried about someone's safety — including yours — I may need to share that with the school's safety team. I want you to know that before we start."

Environment & Timing

Within 3 school days of the incident. 20–45 minutes. Sit at an angle, not directly across. Remove physical barriers between you and the student.

Ground Rules — Read Aloud Verbatim

Rapport-Building Phase · 3–5 min neutral topics

expand_more

"Before we talk about what happened, I'd like to know a little bit about you. Tell me about your family — who lives with you?"

[After family topic, move to school:] "What's your favorite class at school? What do you like about it?"

Minimum requirement: Student produces at least 2–3 complete, multi-sentence responses to open-ended questions before moving to substantive phase. Extend rather than rush.

Practice Narrative · Episodic Memory Training

expand_more

"Now I'd like you to practice telling me a whole story. Tell me everything that happened when you ate breakfast this morning — from the moment you woke up until you got to school. Tell me everything you can remember, in order, from beginning to end."

Use open-ended follow-ups only: "Then what happened?" / "Tell me more." Increases substantive-phase quality by 25–40% (Fisher & Geiselman, 1992).

Interoceptive Vocabulary Scaffold · Reference

expand_more

Ask the open-ended body question first. Wait a minimum of 10 seconds. Only offer a vocabulary menu if the student gives a minimal or vague response. Action-impulse descriptors ("wanted to hit," "wanted to run") are evidence of autonomic state, not behavioral intent.

The Nine Interview Domains

Domains 1–7 are mandatory. Domains 8–9 are conditional — ask when criteria listed on their headers are met. Domain order is not rigid: follow the student when they bring up a topic spontaneously, shift to easier domains if the student dysregulates.
